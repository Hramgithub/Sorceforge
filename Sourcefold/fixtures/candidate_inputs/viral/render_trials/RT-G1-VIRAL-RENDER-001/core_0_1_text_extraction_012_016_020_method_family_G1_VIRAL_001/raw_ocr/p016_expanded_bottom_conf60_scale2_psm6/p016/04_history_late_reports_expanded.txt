In 1958, I/ Culto della Peste dell’Isola Malamente (The Plague
Cult of Malamente Island) was published. Its author, Dr. Giovanni
Pellegrino, claimed to be one of the investigators who shut down
the hospital, and it supposedly described the true story behind the
events in 1954. Before it could hit the shelves however, all copies
of the book were seized by the Italian government and the writer
was arrested on national security grounds. He died in prison a
week later of an unknown wasting disease.

Keeper’s Notes

A few copies of the book escaped destruction, and in fact could
be what draws investigators to the island. The book basically
describes the events that took place on the island in 1954,
though doesn’t suggest anything supernatural in and of itself.

Dr. Pellegrino entered the hospital once the fighting had
ceased, describing the carnage in vivid and disturbing detail.
There are particularly haunting reports of slaughtered patients
with incomprehensible symbols carved into their flesh. He
claims that there were signs of ritual sacrifice, cannibalism,
and unspeakable experiments on women and infants.

He states that the “last stand” in the staff dormitory was as
brutal as any he’d seen in the war, with heavy losses on both
sides. As many as thirteen Italian soldiers had been killed,
with twenty-three monks cut to pieces by machine gun fire.

In the aftermath of the siege, Dr. Pellegrino was tasked with
identifying the dead. He notes that three of the monks were
not found in the hospital, and that they were presumed to
have died attempting to flee the island.

The book has no photos, but there are several drawings
that depict some of the symbols he found.

The Keeper can decide if the investigators can glean anything
relevant to their investigation from the book.

In 1961, the island was deemed off limits to anyone except
with official government permission.

Throughout the 1970s and 80s, people reported seeing
figures and lights on the island, though official reports show
no evidence that anyone has been there in almost 60 years.

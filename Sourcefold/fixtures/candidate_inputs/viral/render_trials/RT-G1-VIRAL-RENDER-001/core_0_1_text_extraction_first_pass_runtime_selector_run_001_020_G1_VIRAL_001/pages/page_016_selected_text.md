## history_continuation

In 1917, a number of soldiers were diagnosed with the flu
and were treated at Saint Roche’s Sanitarium before being
sent to the front. The next year, the 1918 Influenza pandemic
devastated the globe.
In 1928, the facility was upgraded with new medical
equipment and renamed Saint Roche’s Hospital.
During WWII the island remained largely untouched by
the fighting, though a section of the original monastery was
inadvertently damaged by an American bomber when its bomb
release system failed. Rumors were circulated about a secret
mission, though nothing ever came to light.
In 1954, following reports of missing and abused patients,
an investigation by Italian authorities revealed that horrific
experiments were being performed at the hospital. The
remains of hundreds of patients were discovered in cremation
pits and mass graves. The hospital was shut down and dozens
of hospital staff, including several administrators, were
sentenced to life in prison. Strangely, every one of them died
within a year from a mysterious disease that caused them to
waste away. The cause of the illness was never discovered.
Due to the island’s isolation and the possible infectious
nature of the remains, the bodies were never exhumed and
remain on the island to this day. There’s no indication that
there was any sort of cleanup, simply that the island was

Keeper’s Notes
The public is unaware of the cult of Hastalyk, or the intense
firefight that took place when Italian military forces stormed
the island in 1954. When the Italian government first received
information regarding the abuse of patients, it immediately sent
members of the social services to investigate with a police escort.
At first, the hospital administrator seemed cooperative, but when
the investigators discovered fresh remains in the cremation pit,
they ordered the hospital shut down. Subsequently, the social
services investigators and their police escort were lured into the
chapel where they were slaughtered by the cultists, though one
of the police officers did manage to contact their seaplane which
was waiting at the dock. The quick-thinking pilot took off and
managed to radio the authorities.
Meanwhile, the bodies of the investigators were quickly
burned, and realizing that the end was nigh, the cult murdered
the non-cult members of the hospital staff and euthanized all
of the patients. Their remains were brought into the secret
catacombs beneath the chapel.
While this was happening, the authorities reviewed the
pilot’s unsettling report and decided that they would have to
use the military to take the island. Considering the nature of the
situation, they called in the DSS (Divisione Sicurezza Speciale),
a secret division within the Italian government that handles
sensitive issues of national security. In a matter of hours, a
squadron of helicopters was flying to Isola di Malamente with a
heavily armed strike team.
Landing at two points on the island (the lighthouse and the
hospital) the two groups systematically worked their way into

## history_late_reports_expanded

In 1958, I/ Culto della Peste dell’Isola Malamente (The Plague
Cult of Malamente Island) was published. Its author, Dr. Giovanni
Pellegrino, claimed to be one of the investigators who shut down
the hospital, and it supposedly described the true story behind the
events in 1954. Before it could hit the shelves however, all copies
of the book were seized by the Italian government and the writer
was arrested on national security grounds. He died in prison a
week later of an unknown wasting disease.
Keeper’s Notes
A few copies of the book escaped destruction, and in fact could
be what draws investigators to the island. The book basically
describes the events that took place on the island in 1954,
though doesn’t suggest anything supernatural in and of itself.
Dr. Pellegrino entered the hospital once the fighting had
ceased, describing the carnage in vivid and disturbing detail.
There are particularly haunting reports of slaughtered patients
with incomprehensible symbols carved into their flesh. He
claims that there were signs of ritual sacrifice, cannibalism,
and unspeakable experiments on women and infants.
He states that the “last stand” in the staff dormitory was as
brutal as any he’d seen in the war, with heavy losses on both
sides. As many as thirteen Italian soldiers had been killed,
with twenty-three monks cut to pieces by machine gun fire.
In the aftermath of the siege, Dr. Pellegrino was tasked with
identifying the dead. He notes that three of the monks were
not found in the hospital, and that they were presumed to
have died attempting to flee the island.
The book has no photos, but there are several drawings
that depict some of the symbols he found.
The Keeper can decide if the investigators can glean anything
relevant to their investigation from the book.
In 1961, the island was deemed off limits to anyone except
with official government permission.
Throughout the 1970s and 80s, people reported seeing
figures and lights on the island, though official reports show
no evidence that anyone has been there in almost 60 years.

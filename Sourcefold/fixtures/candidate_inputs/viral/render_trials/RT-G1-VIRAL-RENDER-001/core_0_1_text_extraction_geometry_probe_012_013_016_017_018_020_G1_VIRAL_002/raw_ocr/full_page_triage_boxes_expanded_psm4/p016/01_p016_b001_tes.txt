In 1917, a number of soldiers were diagnosed with the flu
and were treated at Saint Roche’s Sanitarium before being
sent to the front. The next year, the 1918 Influenza pandemic
devastated the globe.

In 1928, the facility was upgraded with new medical
equipment and renamed Saint Roche's Hospital.

During WWII the island remained largely untouched by
the fighting, though a section of the original monastery was
inadvertently damaged by an American bomber when its bomb
release system failed. Rumors were circulated about a secret
mission, though nothing ever came to light.

In 1954, following reports of missing and abused patients,
an investigation by Italian authorities revealed that horrific
experiments were being performed at the hospital. The
remains of hundreds of patients were discovered in cremation
pits and mass graves. The hospital was shut down and dozens
of hospital staff, including several administrators, were
sentenced to life in prison. Strangely, every one of them died
within a year from a mysterious disease that caused them to
waste away. The cause of the illness was never discovered.

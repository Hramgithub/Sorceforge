This Sicilian fishing village is fairly typical for the region, and is
full of rustic charm. It derives its name from the plentiful oysters
— and the beautiful pearls they contain — that are regularly
fished in the waters near Perla. When the investigators arrive,
it’s late summer and the village is bedecked in all manner of
colorful flowers in hanging baskets, planters, and window
boxes. All of the buildings are painted white, though time and
the local climate have weathered them quite a bit.

The village is big enough to support a range of shops and
businesses including a baker, butcher, fishmonger, grocery
store, café, and hardware store, most of which are located on
or near the main piazza. The cobblestone roads weave through
Perla, zig-zagging up and down the slope, leading down to the
docks where dozens of fishing boats, tour boats, and various
smaller craft are moored.

Standing above all of the other structures in town is
Cappella di Sant’Anna (Chapel of Saint Anne), a 12th century
church dedicated to the mother of Mary, the patron saint of
childbirth.

The Shops. Exploring the various shops surrounding the
piazza will find typical Sicilian fare. The café is clearly popular
with the elderly men of Perla, many of whom spend the day
chatting, drinking espressos, smoking, and playing cards.
They’re friendly enough, though if asked about the island,
they will become very solemn and quiet, often making hurried
excuses and heading home.

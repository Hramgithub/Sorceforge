## history_text_evidence

```text
History

Sometime during the Stone Age, Hastalyk makes landfall on
Earth from the endless void of space upon what is now known
as Isola di Malamente as a microscopic organism and slowly
begins infecting and defiling the island. The powerful influence
of the Great Old One draws early human settlers there during
the Bronze Age. They begin worshiping this otherworldly, but
invisible horror as a god.

As the Bronze Age settlers make sacrifices to Hastalyk, it
begins to grow in size and eventually manifest as a swirling
charnel pit around the time of what we know as the beginnings
of Ancient Greece around 700 BC. It is still too weak to spread
beyond the island, so instructs its worshipers to build a temple
around it — this is when it was worshiped as Phthisis — a
personification of rot and decay.

Countless temples were built atop the original temple as the
cult adapted to the current pantheons including Erra, Nergal,
Vejovis, Morbus, and eventually the monastery of Saint Roche
was built in the early 14th century just prior to the outbreak of
the Black Death.

The monastery became known for taking in the ill, infirm,
and dying to look after them in their final years. These included
plague victims, lepers, and those suffering from brain fevers
(insanity). The bodies were burned in a large cremation pit. The
monastery was converted into a sanitarium in the late 1800s.
```

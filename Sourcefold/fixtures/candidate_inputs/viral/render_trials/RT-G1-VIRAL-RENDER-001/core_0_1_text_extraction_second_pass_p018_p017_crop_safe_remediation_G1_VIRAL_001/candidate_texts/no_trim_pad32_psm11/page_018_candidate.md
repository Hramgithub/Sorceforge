Welcome to Perla

August 23rd, 2019 — 10am

As you open the van’s sliding door, you’re greeted by a warm

breeze and the mingled scents of bread, fish, lemons, and sea

air. The cobblestone streets are busy with locals going about

their daily business, and as you step out onto the sidewalk, it’s

like stepping into a postcard. Perla is built on the hillside of

a narrow cove overlooking the Mediterranean, and from this

vantage point you have a spectacular view of the entire village.

Looking to the west, you're able to just make out your ultimate

destination on the horizon: Isola di Malamente.

Grateful to stretch your legs, you unload your gear from the

back, drawing a few looks from some nearby children playing

ball. After spending several hours careening over the rough,

twisting roads of the Sicilian countryside in a rickety old van,

all you want now is a shower and maybe a nap.

The white building in front of you looks old, but well kept.

The oval wooden sign overhead creaks and sways in the

pleasant sea breeze, the ornate gold letters flickering in the

sun. It reads Tramoto dell’Hotel (Hotel Sunset). The cab driver

sets down the last of your bags, tips his cap, and rattles away.

The village is abuzz with excitement. As the van pulls away,

the old men stop and stare while some of the women send

some older children over to help you unload their gear. They

point at the hotel asking “Qui?”

The hotel is located right on the town piazza; an eighty

foot circle of cobblestones with a sidewalk along the outside

beneath the wood is blackened and pitted, presumably from

many past fires. Besides the hotel, the piazza is encircled by

a number of shops including a bakery, butcher, fishmonger,

greengrocer, café, and so on.

A handful of old men are working with some of the teenagers

to pack the bonfire with more wood, while several women on

Laughing children chase each other and play all around the

piazza, adding to the general mood of excitement in the air. A

few older women tend the hundreds of sunflowers that reach

for the sky from large planters all around the town square.
